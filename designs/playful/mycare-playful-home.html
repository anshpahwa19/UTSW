                Springs ship as CSS linear() curves sampled from the spring equation (24 steps). Reduced motion swaps every transform for a 240 ms cross-fade: no bounce, scale, parallax, or looping motion, and icon fills change instantly. The emergency alert only ever fades or reveals in height.

                Iconography

                One rounded icon family, tuned to the MyCare scale

                Every icon comes from Material Symbols Rounded, a variable icon font. Icons are outlined at rest and fill when they are selected, pressed, or confirmed. Destinations sit in brand dots; controls and clinical details stay plain.

                Why this family

                • Rounded ends and corners echo the round-capped U in the MyCare symbol and the app's 24.0–32.0 radii.
                • Four variable axes – fill, weight, grade, and optical size – so states animate without swapping files, and strokes look even from 16.0 to 32.0.
                • The broadest open healthcare vocabulary: stethoscope, lab report, test tubes, pill, video visit, and wait times.
                • One source for iOS and Android under the Apache 2.0 license, trimmed to the 44 glyphs MyCare uses (66 KB).

                Also considered: SF Symbols (licensed for Apple platforms only), Phosphor (no variable axes, smaller healthcare set), and a custom set (most ownable, highest cost – keep it for brand-only glyphs later, drawn on this grid).

                Glyphs that carry meaning keep at least 3:1 against their dot. Never put cyan, orange, or green glyphs on white – they fall below 3:1.

                Avoid

                • local_hospital

                  Crosses as medical shorthand. A red cross on white is a protected emblem (Geneva Conventions; 18 U.S.C. § 706), and any cross reads as first aid.

                • emergency

                  Asterisks and Star of Life shapes. They signal ambulances and emergency departments, not urgent care.

                • syringe

                  Needles, unless the content is about vaccines. They can raise anxiety.

                • ecg_heart

                  Heartbeat lines as decoration. A cliché that implies cardiology.

                Also avoid emoji, outline and filled glyphs mixed at rest, icon-only status, a second icon family, and sizes off the ramp (18.0, 22.0, 28.0).

                Accessibility

                • Icons beside text are decorative and hidden from screen readers, so ligature names like “calendar_add_on” are never read aloud.
                • Icon-only buttons carry a name: “Notifications, two new,” “Close search,” “Next appointment.”
                • Targets stay 44.0 or larger, at least 8.0 apart, whatever the glyph size.
                • Glyphs that carry meaning keep 3:1 against their background; text labels keep 4.5:1.
                • State changes shape (outline to filled), and the label or selected state says it too.
                • Icons scale with text size. Until the icon font loads, glyphs stay invisible instead of showing their names.

                Engineering handoff

                • Concepts, not glyphs. Screens and the CMS refer to a concept key such as findCare or addCal; the glyph map resolves it. Admins pick tile icons from this approved list – no uploads.
                • One subset font. Ship only the mapped glyphs in one woff2 (66 KB with all four axes) bundled with the app, not fetched at runtime.
                • Axes. 'FILL' 0|1, 'wght' 500, 'GRAD' 0|-25, 'opsz' 20–32. Animate FILL for state changes.
                • Native. iOS and Android load the same variable font, or export vectors at FILL 0 and 1 named ic_find_care_outline and ic_find_care_filled.
