UTSW MyCare home – interactive direction

High-fidelity prototype of the patient app home screen. A Blue hero puts the greeting and search first – search always sits above appointments – and the content rides up on a light sheet. Appointments get a date strip and a rich card with a countdown, map preview, and round actions. The energy comes from motion and gestures. All people, dates, and alerts are fictional sample data.

              Iconography

              One rounded icon family, one color, tuned to the MyCare scale

              Every icon comes from Material Symbols Rounded, a variable icon font, and every icon is UT Southwestern Blue – or white on Blue. Icons are outlined at rest and fill when they are selected, pressed, or confirmed. Destinations and actions sit in circles; controls and clinical details stay plain.

              Why this family

              • Rounded ends and corners echo the round-capped U in the MyCare symbol.
              • Four variable axes – fill, weight, grade, and optical size – so states animate without swapping files, and strokes look even from 16.0 to 32.0.
              • The broadest open healthcare vocabulary: stethoscope, lab report, test tubes, pill, video visit, and wait times.
              • One source for iOS and Android under the Apache 2.0 license, trimmed to the 52 glyphs MyCare uses (74 KB).

              Also considered: SF Symbols (licensed for Apple platforms only), Phosphor (no variable axes, smaller healthcare set), and a custom set (most ownable, highest cost – keep it for brand-only glyphs later, drawn on this grid).

              Icons carry one color – Blue, or white on Blue. No per-icon colors: they read as decoration, compete with the alert red, and make a medical app feel like a toy.

              Avoid

              • local_hospital

                Crosses as medical shorthand. A red cross on white is a protected emblem (Geneva Conventions; 18 U.S.C. § 706), and any cross reads as first aid.

              • emergency

                Asterisks and Star of Life shapes. They signal ambulances and emergency departments, not urgent care.

              • syringe

                Needles, unless the content is about vaccines. They can raise anxiety.

              • ecg_heart

                Heartbeat lines as decoration. A cliché that implies cardiology.

              Also avoid a different color for each icon, emoji, outline and filled glyphs mixed at rest, icon-only status, a second icon family, and sizes off the ramp (18.0, 22.0, 28.0).

              Accessibility

              • Icons beside text are decorative and hidden from screen readers, so ligature names like “calendar_add_on” are never read aloud.
              • Icon-only buttons carry a name: “Notifications, two unread,” “Close search,” “Refresh home.”
              • Targets stay 44.0 or larger, at least 8.0 apart, whatever the glyph size.
              • Glyphs that carry meaning keep 3:1 against their background; text keeps 4.5:1.
              • State changes shape (outline to filled) and container color, and the label or selected state says it too.
              • Icons scale with text size. Until the icon font loads, glyphs stay invisible instead of showing their names.

              Engineering handoff

              • Concepts, not glyphs. Screens and the CMS refer to a concept key such as findCare or addCal; the glyph map resolves it. Admins pick tile icons from this approved list – no uploads, no colors.
              • One subset font. Ship only the mapped glyphs in one woff2 (74 KB with all four axes) bundled with the app, not fetched at runtime.
              • Axes. 'FILL' 0|1, 'wght' 500, 'GRAD' 0|-25, 'opsz' 20–32. Animate FILL for state changes.
              • Native. iOS and Android load the same variable font, or export vectors at FILL 0 and 1 named ic_find_care_outline and ic_find_care_filled.
