    Five scenarios on one home

    Pick a scenario above the journey. 1 Guest nearby: at a campus, everyone sees care near you sorted by walking time, open times, and facilities; each place opens with directions and what is there – the café menu, lab and imaging prices, pharmacy items, chapel times, parking rates. 2 Appointments: nothing is booked in the app – a visit already in MyChart shows first; otherwise doctors show open times, for information. 3 Why you're here: with no visit today, the home asks once on arrival; each answer reshapes the top of the home, and Change is always there. 4 3D wayfinding: inside a building the app becomes the map – all floors pulled apart with your route through them, categories that fly to the nearest place with Start navigation, and turn-by-turn that walks you, rides the elevator, and switches to Live view. 5 Section guides: walk into a section – the lobby, imaging, the lab, rehab, the heart center – and its guide unlocks: the map lights up that section and the home leads with what is only there.

      One line, many places

      The home is the day: a line of stops from leaving home to the last visit. The cyan ring is you. As the simulated location changes, the ring travels down the line, the passed stops turn to checks, and the next stop opens with only what that place needs. Search, Quick links, the tabs, and the menu sit in the same place in every state.

        A journey step on the board turns location on if it was never asked. The app itself never asks on launch: it explains first, then shows the system prompt.

        Trust and control

        • The location chip is always on top: it says what the home is using and why. Tap it for Not here?, Show the common homepage, and Location settings.
        • Without location, everything still works: choose a building, a spot inside it, or a ZIP or city.
        • The lock screen shows live directions – the place, floor, suite, and step – but never the department or the visit. Turn it on under Moments and press Next to watch it advance. Visits, check-in, and details open in MyChart.

        One 3D map system

        A live WebGL map (three.js r128, loaded from cdnjs) renders the campus, every floor, and directions: towers with glass facades, layered garages with parked cars, rooms with walls, doors, and furniture, soft shadows, and labels that never collide. Drag to turn it. One renderer moves between the home and directions; card maps are still images of it. If WebGL is not available, the same scenes fall back to the drawing below.

        The fallback draws campus and floor plans with one tilted, extruded renderer: rooms and buildings rise as blocks, departments get callouts, amenities get round badges, the route is a bold line with moving dashes, you are a blue dot with a heading cone, and the destination gets a pin. Rooms are drawn from the same data as the tiles and directory, so a room on the map always matches its tile. You and the route stay on roads, walkways, and corridors – never on a wall or a label. The “Sample map” tag lives in its own footer band.
